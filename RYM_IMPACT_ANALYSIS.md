# What the RateYourMusic import added

Generated by `scripts/analyze_rym_impact.py`. Import marker date: **2026-09-13** (every appended RYM row carries the import date, not a listening date).

Two halves of one file:

| | rows | rated | mean | median | sd | % >= 90 | % <= 30 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| RYM import | 2546 | 2546 | 65.8 | 70 | 15.1 | 3.8% | 5.3% |
| Blog corpus | 2554 | 2554 | 77.4 | 83 | 21.1 | 25.4% | 5.8% |
| Combined | 5100 | 5100 | 71.6 | 70 | 19.3 | 14.6% | 5.5% |

The import is **50% of the rated collection** — it is not a side file, it is half the dataset, and it moved the collection average from **77.4 to 71.6**.

> Housekeeping: the README headline still reads "3,214 rated songs · 1,601 artists". The real figures are now **5100 rated songs** across **3161 distinct artists**. Worth a one-line update so the docs stop contradicting the data.

## 1. The two halves use different rating scales

This is the single most important finding. The RYM half is not 'worse music', it is a **different ruler**. RYM ratings are 1–10 multiplied by 10, so they arrive in 10-point steps and 53% of them land on 70:

| RYM rating | rows | share | same percentile on the blog scale | shift |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 55 | 2.2% | 1 | -9 |
| 20 | 51 | 2.0% | 1 | -19 |
| 30 | 28 | 1.1% | 18 | -12 |
| 40 | 67 | 2.6% | 40 | +0 |
| 50 | 165 | 6.5% | 60 | +10 |
| 60 | 397 | 15.6% | 72 | +12 |
| 70 | 1352 | 53.1% | 85 | +15 |
| 77 | 2 | 0.1% | 91 | +14 |
| 80 | 331 | 13.0% | 94 | +14 |
| 90 | 66 | 2.6% | 100 | +10 |
| 100 | 32 | 1.3% | 100 | +0 |

Independent check: for the **283 artists rated in both halves**, the blog average sits **+12.7 points** above the RYM average (median +12.5). So a RYM 7/10 is roughly a blog 85, and a RYM 8/10 is roughly a blog 94.

**Consequences** — read these before trusting any chart that mixes the halves:

- The dashboard average (71.6) is a blend of two scales, not a taste measurement.
- 'Songs rated 90+' now means two different things depending on which half the row came from.
- Genre averages computed on the merged file are dragged toward whatever genre the RYM half over-samples (see §2).
- Artist-vs-artist comparisons are unfair whenever one artist is mostly RYM and the other is mostly blog.

**Suggested fix (no data loss):** keep the raw rating, add a `source` column (`rym` / `blog`), and expose a *calibrated* rating (`rating_calibrated`) that maps RYM onto the blog scale with the table above. Everything user-facing reads the calibrated value; the raw value stays for audit.

## 3. What the import actually added

- **1552 artists you had never rated before**, out of 1835 in the RYM half — only **283 artists** appear in both halves. The import roughly doubled the artist roster rather than deepening the old one.
- **Deeper back catalog.** Share of songs released before 2000:
  **36%** in the RYM half vs **13%** in the blog half.
- **More non-English titles:** 4.8% vs 2.5% (CJK 114 vs 57).
- **Nearly disjoint songs:** only 5 artist+song pairs exist in both halves, so this is almost pure new listening, not a re-rating exercise.

- **Film / game scores are your single strongest genre.** Soundtrack/Score is the top genre in the RYM half (z = +0.94) and comfortably above average in the blog half too. The 100s in the import are dominated by Morricone, John Williams, Hisaishi, Giacchino and HOYO-MiX — this is a real, load-bearing preference, not noise.
- **Rap/Hip-Hop is a genuine blind spot**, but the dashboard exaggerates it. The blog half contains 45 rows from the deliberate 'rate 20 famous rap songs at 1/100' experiment (avg 6.1). Strip those and the blog rap average is **60.8** (n=146), and the independent RYM half says **51.8** (n=97). Both are your lowest genre — so the conclusion holds, but the true gap is roughly 10 points, not 30.
- **Country and Latin sit at the bottom of both halves.** No recovery signal.
- **Japan and Korea punch above their weight in both halves** (see §6).

## 7. Data-quality issues the import introduced or exposed

1. **Two scales in one column** (§1). Highest-impact fix in this report.
2. **Import date used as review date** (§2). Breaks the Evolution view.
3. **563 rows (22%) are releases, not songs** — the song field contains ' / ', i.e. a single/B-side pair, a medley or an EP side (`Layla / Bell Bottom Blues`, `Dream On / Somebody`). A further 12 look like album or OST rows (`Queen — A Night at the Opera`, `Billie Eilish — Hit Me Hard and Soft`, `Genshin Impact: City of Winds and Idylls`). Several of your RYM 100s are albums, so 'top songs' lists will surface release titles. This matches the caveat already in `data/rym_import_summary.md`; it is now quantified.
4. **Genre classifier strain.** Uncategorized went from 2.5% (blog) to 4.3% (RYM). Worse, the RYM **Metal** bucket is polluted: it contains Leonard Cohen, Jessie Ware, Maxwell, Nico, Burial, Big Thief, Weyes Blood, The National, Japan, George Benson, Charlie Parker, Grace Jones, Kavinsky, Modjo, Steve Lacy, Janelle Monáe, Howard Shore and Jeremy Soule. Those artists are cached as Metal in `data/artist_genre_cache.json` (191 artists total). The likely mechanism is the bidirectional substring rule in `TasteEngine._classify_artist_genre_musicbrainz` (`if kw in t or t in kw`): a MusicBrainz tag like `folk`, `glam`, `core`, `prog` or `power` is a *substring* of the keywords `folk metal`, `glam metal`, `metalcore`, `prog metal`, `power metal`, so Metal collects spurious votes. The blog half hides this because it never had those artists; the import exposed it. **This is why the Metal 'sign flip' in §4 is not a taste change.**
5. **Joke / novelty rows** in the low tail (§5e).
6. **96 blog rows rated 1–2**, 45 of them from the deliberate rap experiment. Flagging that experiment with a `source` value would stop it from silently defining your rap preference.

## 8. Suggested next steps (ordered by value)

1. Add a `source` column (`blog` / `rym` / `experiment`) and a `rating_calibrated` column. Backfill `source` from the import date; derive calibration from the §1 table. No rows are lost and every view can pick the right column.
2. Exclude import-dated rows from the Evolution/timeline series.
3. Fix the substring rule in `_classify_artist_genre_musicbrainz` to match keywords only in the tag→keyword direction, then re-run genre classification for the artists currently cached as Metal and audit the diff.
4. Split the ' / ' release rows into a `row_kind` of `song` / `release` and exclude `release` from song-level stats and recommender training.
5. Re-check the two known suspect fills already noted in `data/rym_import_summary.md` (TUYU, Mitchie M) and the single-song contradictions in §5c.
6. Promote the §5d artists into `favorite_artists.json` after a listen — Tori Amos, Garbage and Yeah Yeah Yeahs are the strongest new candidates.
